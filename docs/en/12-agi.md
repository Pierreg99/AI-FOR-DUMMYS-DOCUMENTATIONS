# Artificial general intelligence

Treat AGI as a question about capabilities and measurement, rather than a product label or fixed architecture.

## Learning goal

Break an AGI claim into testable questions. Depending on the definition, artificial general intelligence describes broad and capable artificial intelligence. Definitions differ in the tasks, performance levels, and forms of autonomy they require.

## Three separate questions

| Dimension | Claim to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Performance | How well does the system perform on specified tasks? |
| Generality | How broad and transferable are its capabilities? |
| Autonomy | Which tasks can it perform independently, under what conditions? |

A system may write many kinds of text yet fail at a simple interaction. A highly autonomous process may be narrowly specialized. Neither parameter count nor tool access defines AGI on its own.

## Worked example

A vendor calls a system “generally intelligent” because it solves five benchmarks. Ask about unfamiliar tasks, data overlap, tools, human assistance, and failed attempts. Investigate whether improvements in one area come with regressions elsewhere. Record the evaluation environment so the claim does not silently expand beyond the tested conditions.

## Limits and open questions

Scientific operationalizations differ; they do not establish one universally accepted pass threshold. Benchmarks are samples of human-defined tasks. Strong performance also does not automatically answer questions about consciousness, intentions, or long-term controllability.

This documentation therefore treats AGI as a research and capability concept. The engineering chapters describe testable system components without claiming that assembling them guarantees a path to AGI. Distinguish a proposed definition from evidence that a particular system meets it.

## Exercise

“Our agent can close a ticket independently, so it is AGI.” What inference is missing?

## Answer and self-check

One autonomous task does not demonstrate broad, transferable capability. An explicit definition and meaningful tests across different unfamiliar tasks and conditions would be needed. Even then, the claim applies within the chosen evaluation framework.
